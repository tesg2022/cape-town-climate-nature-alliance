Who we are

A collaborative platform for climate and nature research.

CTCNA brings complementary disciplines together to understand environmental change and strengthen the evidence behind climate-related decisions.

Our work connects climate science, hydrology, environmental modelling, geospatial analytics, biodiversity, public participation and environmental governance. We aim to support researchers, communities, public institutions and decision-makers with accessible, transparent and decision-relevant knowledge.

Based in Cape Town, the Alliance focuses on locally relevant challenges while building methods and partnerships that can inform climate resilience across the Western Cape, South Africa and wider regions.

Our enabling platform

ClimateNatureAI turns environmental data into decision-ready insight.

ClimateNatureAI is the Alliance’s integrated environmental intelligence and assessment platform. It is designed to connect location-based climate, water, flood, ecosystem and nature-based solution information with predictive analysis and auditable reporting.

✓Coordinate-based environmental profiles
✓Environmental vulnerability screening
✓Predictive and scenario analysis
✓GIS-ready and reproducible outputs
✓Stakeholder and field-observation workflows
✓Transparent assumptions and uncertainty

The platform is being advanced through staged integration of validated Earth-observation and environmental monitoring datasets.

Flagship initiative

Responsible climate intervention assessment in Cape Town.

CTCNA’s proposed CIEIF-aligned initiative places environmental oversight, predictive impact assessment and public participation before any field deployment.

ClimateNatureAI for environmental oversight

The initiative proposes to use ClimateNatureAI to assess environmental, hydrological, ecological, social and governance implications associated with a proposed soil-moisture preservation and carbon-sequestration intervention, including biochar-enhanced biodegradable hydrogel composites as a case study.

  • Environmental baseline and impact assessment
  • Predictive environmental and social modelling
  • Public-health and ecological risk screening
  • Regulatory pathway and permit assessment
  • Monitoring, reporting and verification planning

Scientific transparency: These figures describe the supplied preliminary assessment files, not a complete municipal monitoring network. The project summary flags the City boundary as not yet validated and the intervention as not yet specified. Modelled soil-moisture relationships do not establish causal intervention effects. Community exposure, public-health impacts, permits and intervention-specific outcomes require additional datasets, specialist review and field evidence.

The supplied assessment archive reports a Random Forest soil-moisture model evaluated using spatial group holdout (R² ≈ 0.95; MAE ≈ 0.35; RMSE ≈ 0.57 in the source data’s soil-moisture units). These are preliminary model metrics and should be interpreted alongside the limited sample size, data provenance and validation limitations.

Responsible research

Trust, safeguards and accountability are part of the science.

Climate intervention and nature-based approaches must be assessed with care. CTCNA’s approach places environmental integrity, transparent evidence and meaningful participation at the centre of research planning.
